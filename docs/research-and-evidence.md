# Research and Evidence Standard

## Source priority

1. Primary documents: official documentation, research papers, filings, datasets, policy documents, direct statements.
2. Credible independent reporting and expert analysis.
3. Practitioner and community discussion for discovering questions, use cases, and sentiment.
4. Search trends and platform analytics for demand signals.

A source's existence does not automatically validate every claim it contains.

## Record each source

Capture title, author/organisation, publisher, URL, publication date, access date, source type, supported claims, relevant excerpts or notes, limitations, and conflicts of interest.

## Claim categories

- **Fact:** directly supported by reliable evidence.
- **Attributed claim:** explicitly attributed to a person or organisation.
- **Analysis:** interpretation based on evidence.
- **Scenario:** conditional possibility.
- **Speculation:** uncertain possibility; label clearly or omit.

## Verification rules

- Verify numbers, dates, names, quotes, and causal claims.
- Prefer original sources for important assertions.
- Cross-check consequential or disputed claims.
- Distinguish announcement from independently verified result.
- Preserve uncertainty and conflicting evidence.
- Never invent a citation or imply a source says more than it does.

## Research brief exit criteria

A brief is ready when the audience question, editorial angle, source set, key claims, evidence gaps, uncertainty, and proposed payoff are explicit.
